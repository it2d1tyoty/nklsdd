What Is Contrave?

Approved by the Food & Drug Administration in 2014, Contrave is an oral, twice-daily medication that’s a combination of two drugs: naltrexone and bupropion (1).

“Both medications [naltrexone and bupropion] have been around for a long time,” says endocrinologist Sarah Fishman, M.D., Ph.D., adding that neither was designed specifically for weight loss, but when combined they have been found to help you lose weight.

Here’s a quick breakdown of what naltrexone and bupropion are so that you understand how, when combined together, they help you shed pounds.

Naltrexone

This drug is known as an opioid receptor antagonist, and it’s used to treat alcohol or opioid use disorder, Fishman explains. “This drug blocks the reward pathway you get from comfort eating, making it effective for people who struggle with emotional or mindless eating,” she says.

How Does Contrave Help You Lose Weight?

The weight loss drug aids weight loss by reducing emotionally driven food cravings and suppressing appetite. “Contrave helps patients focus on what their issues are around food. It also makes it easier to stick to your diet goals and improve your eating habits,” says Fishman. That’s because both naltrexone and bupropion can help control feelings of pleasure you get when eating. In part, this can also help manage unhealthy food cravings.

If you commonly eat in response to stress or emotions, you may no longer feel that way when taking Contrave. So, if you’re at a restaurant, you can make a more clear-headed choice in the fish over the mac ‘n cheese. Fishman often prescribes Contrave to folks hoping to achieve modest weight loss, or about five to 10 percent of their body weight. Early research on the drug demonstrated its efficacy to help people hit this weight loss goal.

In one study of around 1,500 obese adults, people who took the drug lost, on average, 6.5 percent of their body weight after 28 weeks—compared to the placebo group who lost an average of 1.9 percent (both groups were eating a calorie-restricted diet and were instructed to exercise more) (2). More specifically, 56 percent of those who took Contrave lost at least 5 percent of their body weight, compared to just 17 percent in the placebo group.

Do You Need a Prescription for Contrave?

Yes, your physician will need to prescribe Contrave to you. Contrave is only available if you have a BMI of 30, which qualifies as obese. This is a weight loss medication that can cause side effects. Most commonly those are mild, like nausea, headache, and constipation, but they can still be disruptive to your life. Contrave May Not be the Drug For You

Not all doctors remain optimistic about Contrave, especially in light of other options, such as GLP-1 medications like Wegovy.

“For the patients I see, the root of the problem is their metabolism,” says Sue Decottis, M.D., a board-certified weight loss specialist. She prefers GLP-1s because of the benefits to insulin resistance, which she finds is a bigger barrier to weight loss.

After you eat, your blood sugar naturally rises in order to signal the pancreas to pump out insulin, which is a hormone that pushes blood sugar into cells to be used for energy (5). A GLP-1 drug—which mimics the natural GLP-1 hormone in your body—causes the body to produce more insulin, which then lowers blood sugar levels (6).
