Symptoms of Common STIs: A Clinical Visual Guide to Identification

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Common STI symptoms include unusual discharge, burning during urination, genital sores or blisters, rashes on the palms or soles, pelvic or testicular pain, itching, unusual odor, rectal discomfort, and flu-like illness after possible exposure.

Because STI symptoms often overlap—and many infections cause no symptoms at all—testing is the only way to confirm what's happening. Early diagnosis and treatment protect both your health and your partners'. Seek urgent care for severe pelvic pain, high fever, a rapidly spreading rash, neurological changes, or sudden testicular swelling.

Explanation

Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are common, and many are treatable—especially when identified early. Understanding STI symptoms can help you recognize when something may be wrong and prompt timely care. This guide uses clear, plain language and clinically accurate descriptions to help you understand what different STIs may look and feel like. While visuals can be helpful, symptoms can vary widely by person, anatomy, and stage of infection. If anything here feels familiar or concerning, it's important to speak to a doctor—especially if symptoms are severe, persistent, or could be life-threatening.

Important note: Some STIs cause no symptoms at all. Regular screening is essential if you're sexually active.

Chlamydia

Overview: Chlamydia is one of the most common bacterial STIs and is often silent.

Common STI symptoms may include:

Unusual vaginal or penile discharge (often clear or cloudy)

Burning or pain during urination

Pelvic or lower abdominal pain

Pain during sex

Testicular pain or swelling (less common)

Rectal pain, discharge, or bleeding (with anal exposure)

Gonorrhea

Overview: Gonorrhea often occurs alongside chlamydia and may cause more noticeable symptoms.

Thick, yellow or green discharge from the penis or vagina

Pain or burning during urination

Increased vaginal bleeding between periods

Testicular pain or swelling

Rectal discomfort, discharge, or bleeding

Sore throat (with oral exposure)

Visual clues:Discharge is often more pronounced and may stain underwear.

Why it matters: Untreated gonorrhea can cause joint infections, PID, and infertility.

Syphilis

Overview: Syphilis develops in stages, each with different symptoms.

Stage 1 (Primary):

A single, firm, painless sore (chancre) at the site of infection

Swollen lymph nodes

Stage 2 (Secondary):

Rash (often on palms of hands or soles of feet)

Fever, fatigue, sore throat

Patchy hair loss

Flat, wart-like growths in the genital area

Later stages:

Damage to the brain, nerves, heart, or other organs

Visual clues:The initial sore can be easy to miss because it doesn't hurt.

Why it matters: Syphilis can be life-threatening if untreated. Early treatment is very effective.

Genital Herpes (HSV-1 and HSV-2)

Overview: Herpes is a viral STI that remains in the body for life, with periods of flare-ups.

Tingling or itching before sores appear

Small blisters that break open into painful ulcers

Pain during urination

Flu-like symptoms during the first outbreak

Visual clues:Clusters of fluid-filled blisters or open sores around the genitals, anus, or mouth.

Why it matters: While not curable, herpes is manageable with antiviral medication.

Human Papillomavirus (HPV)

Overview: HPV is extremely common. Many types cause no symptoms and clear on their own.

Genital warts (soft, flesh-colored bumps; flat or cauliflower-like)

Often no symptoms at all

Visual clues:Warts may appear weeks or months after exposure and can be very small.

Why it matters: Some HPV types can lead to cervical, anal, throat, or penile cancers. Regular screening is key.

Trichomoniasis

Overview: A parasitic STI that is more common than many realize.

Frothy, yellow-green vaginal discharge

Strong vaginal odor

Vaginal itching or irritation

Pain during urination or sex

Penile irritation or discharge (often mild or absent)

Visual clues:In clinical exams, the cervix may appear red or inflamed.

Why it matters: It increases the risk of acquiring or transmitting other STIs.

HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus)

Overview: HIV affects the immune system and may not cause symptoms for years.

Early symptoms (2–4 weeks after exposure):

Fever

Sore throat

Rash

Fatigue

Later symptoms (if untreated):

Recurrent infections

Weight loss

Night sweats

Visual clues:Early rashes are often flat and widespread, not itchy.

Why it matters: Early diagnosis and treatment allow people with HIV to live long, healthy lives.

When Symptoms Overlap or Are Absent

Many STI symptoms look alike—or don't appear at all. Discharge, sores, itching, and pain can also be caused by non-STI conditions such as yeast infections or skin irritation. That's why testing matters more than visual inspection alone.

Emotional and Psychological Considerations

Experiencing genital symptoms can be stressful. If your symptoms follow an unwanted sexual experience, you may also notice emotional signs such as anxiety, sleep problems, or avoidance. When to Speak to a Doctor Immediately

You should speak to a doctor or seek urgent care if you experience:

Severe pelvic or abdominal pain

High fever

Rapidly spreading rash

Neurological symptoms (vision changes, weakness, confusion)

Painful swelling of the testicles

Any symptom that feels sudden, severe, or life-threatening

Key Takeaways

STI symptoms vary widely and may be mild, severe, or absent.

Visual signs can guide concern but cannot confirm a diagnosis.

Early testing and treatment protect your health and your partners.
