Wegovy (semaglutide) has become a popular prescription for chronic weight management, but many users report digestive side effects—diarrhea being one of the most common. Why Does Wegovy Cause Diarrhea?

Wegovy belongs to a class of drugs called GLP-1 receptor agonists. These medications mimic a natural hormone (glucagon-like peptide-1) that helps regulate appetite and blood sugar. Here's how GLP-1 activity can lead to diarrhea:

Slowed gastric emptyingGLP-1 receptor agonists delay the time it takes for food to move from your stomach into the small intestine. This can sometimes disrupt normal digestive rhythms, leading to loose stools.

Increased intestinal fluid secretionSemaglutide can promote fluid secretion in the small intestine. An excess of fluid in the bowel makes stool less formed.

Altered gut motilityBy affecting nerve signals in the gut, GLP-1 drugs can speed up or irregularize contractions in the intestines, resulting in diarrhea.

Changes in gut microbiotaEarly research suggests that GLP-1 receptor agonists may shift the balance of bacteria in your gut, which could influence stool consistency.

Who's Most at Risk?

Not everyone on Wegovy will develop diarrhea. Factors that may increase your risk include:

Rapid dose escalation: Jumping to higher doses too quickly may overwhelm your digestive system.

Prior GI sensitivity: A history of irritable bowel syndrome or chronic loose stools can make you more prone to diarrhea.

Dietary habits: High-fat, greasy, or very spicy meals can exacerbate diarrhea when combined with Wegovy.

Medications and supplements: Certain antibiotics, magnesium supplements, or sugar alcohols (found in some "diet" foods) can add to loose stools.

If you've just started Wegovy or recently increased your dose, mild diarrhea is often temporary. Try these practical steps to reduce symptoms:

Dietary Adjustments

Introduce well-cooked potatoes, plain crackers, or low-fat yogurt.

Limit fat and fiber extremes:

Very high-fiber foods (like raw vegetables, beans) can worsen diarrhea.

Excessive fat (fried foods, full-fat dairy) can also be harsh on your system.

Medication Timing and Dosage

Slow dose titration

Work with your prescriber to increase the semaglutide dose gradually, allowing your gut to adapt.

Injection timing

Some people find injecting earlier in the day (rather than evening) gives their digestive tract more time to process food before bedtime.

Over-the-Counter and Supplement Options

Loperamide or bismuth subsalicylate

Short-term use can control acute diarrhea. Follow package instructions and check with your doctor before starting.

Probiotics

Strains like Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium may help restore healthy gut flora balance.

Soluble fiber

Psyllium husk or ground chia seeds can bulk up stools. Start with a small amount and increase slowly.

When to Worry

Most episodes of Wegovy-related diarrhea are mild and self-limited. Monitor for these warning signs, which warrant prompt medical attention:

Signs of dehydration: Dizziness, dry mouth, dark urine, rapid heartbeat

Severe abdominal pain or cramping that doesn't improve with OTC remedies

Fever above 100.4°F (38°C)

Blood or mucus in your stool

Persistent diarrhea lasting more than 48–72 hours

Unintended weight loss beyond your prescribed goal

Should You Switch Medications?

Deciding whether to stay on Wegovy or move to another treatment depends on several factors:

Symptom severity and duration

Occasional loose stools during dose increases often improve within weeks.

Chronic, disruptive diarrhea that interferes with your daily life may justify reconsideration.

Impact on weight-loss goals

If diarrhea leads to poor appetite, electrolyte imbalances, or nutrient deficiencies, it could hinder your overall progress.

Alternative options

Other GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., liraglutide) may have slightly different side-effect profiles.
