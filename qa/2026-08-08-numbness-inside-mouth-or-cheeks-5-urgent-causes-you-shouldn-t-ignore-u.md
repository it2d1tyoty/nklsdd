Internal Medicine

Numbness in the mouth, particularly in the cheeks, is most often caused by nerve-related issues or trauma from recent dental procedures. Other possible causes include infections, allergic reactions, vitamin deficiencies, or underlying neurological conditions. Because the trigeminal nerve controls sensation in the face and mouth, any irritation, inflammation, or injury to this nerve can trigger numbness, tingling, or a "pins and needles" feeling in the cheeks.

Since cheek numbness can stem from many different causes—some minor, others requiring prompt medical attention—it's important to identify the likely reason before deciding on next steps.
