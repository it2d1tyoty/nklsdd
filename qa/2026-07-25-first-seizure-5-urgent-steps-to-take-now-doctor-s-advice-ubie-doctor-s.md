What should I do after experiencing my first seizure?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

A first seizure requires prompt medical evaluation to determine the cause and reduce the risk of recurrence. Key steps include:

See a healthcare provider quickly for a thorough neurological assessment, which may include an EEG, MRI, or blood tests.

Follow safety precautions, such as avoiding driving, swimming alone, operating heavy machinery, or climbing heights until cleared by a doctor.

Track symptoms and triggers, including sleep loss, stress, missed meals, or flashing lights.

Inform close contacts so they know how to respond if another seizure occurs.

Because a first seizure can signal a wide range of underlying conditions—from temporary triggers to epilepsy—understanding your symptoms early is critical. Seek medical evaluation: Visit a doctor or neurologist as soon as possible to identify the cause and determine if treatment is needed. You can start by using a free symptom checker to help you understand your symptoms before your appointment. Avoid driving and risky activities: Do not drive, operate heavy machinery, or swim alone until cleared by your doctor. Follow-up tests: Your doctor may order EEG, brain imaging (like MRI or CT), and blood tests to find the cause. Medication: Depending on the cause and risk of recurrence, you may be prescribed anti-seizure medications. Inform close contacts: Let family, friends, and coworkers know about your seizure and what to do if you have another. Safety at home: Remove hazards that could cause injury during a seizure, such as sharp objects or unsecured furniture.

Early medical care and support can improve outcomes and help you manage your condition safely.
