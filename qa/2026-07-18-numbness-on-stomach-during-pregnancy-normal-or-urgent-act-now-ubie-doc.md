Is numbness on the stomach normal during pregnancy, and what should I do about it?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Yes, stomach numbness during pregnancy is common and typically occurs as the skin stretches and nerves become compressed to accommodate a growing baby. Most women experience this sensation in the second and third trimesters, and it usually resolves after delivery.

Explanation

During pregnancy, your body changes in many ways. The growing uterus changes how your muscles and nerves on the stomach and back work. Sometimes, this extra stretching and pressure can make you feel numb or tingly in your stomach area. For example, research has shown that working postures and the extra load on the spinal column and abdominal muscles can play a role in these sensations. In some cases, the pressure may put stress on nerves, leading to numbness, similar to what happens in other peripheral nerves during pregnancy.

It is important to remember that while some numbness can be normal, especially if it is mild and not accompanied by other concerning symptoms like pain or weakness, you should still mention it during your prenatal checkups. Your doctor can help determine if this feeling is a common change during pregnancy or if further evaluation is needed. If you're experiencing unusual numbness or other symptoms and want to better understand what might be causing them, you can use a free symptom checker to help identify possible causes before your next appointment. If the numbness begins to affect your daily activities or you feel worried about it, please make sure to reach out to your healthcare provider for personalized advice.
