If you've started Ozempic (semaglutide) and are experiencing sudden diarrhea, you're not alone. Gastrointestinal side effects are common with this medication. Why Ozempic Can Trigger Diarrhea

Ozempic is a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist prescribed for type 2 diabetes and sometimes weight management. It works by:

Slowing stomach emptying

Increasing feelings of fullness

Regulating blood sugar

Altered gut motility

Increased intestinal secretions

Before trying any remedies, gauge how severe your diarrhea is:

24-Hour Plan to Calm Your Gut

Hour 1–3: Stabilize Hydration

Diarrhea can rapidly deplete fluids and electrolytes. To rehydrate:

Sip clear liquids slowly: water, broth, or an electrolyte solution (e.g., oral rehydration salts).

Avoid caffeine and alcohol—they can worsen diarrhea.

Aim for small sips every 10–15 minutes rather than large gulps.

Hour 4–8: Gentle Rest and Nutrition

Give your digestive system a break:

Rest in a comfortable, seated or semi-reclined position.

When hunger returns, start with the BRAT diet:

Bananas (potassium)

Rice (binding starch)

Applesauce (pectin)

Toast (low-fiber carbohydrates)

Avoid high-fat, greasy, or spicy foods.

Do not introduce dairy products yet—lactose intolerance can be temporary after diarrhea.

Hour 8–16: Gradual Reintroduction of Nutritious Foods

If your stools are forming and you feel better:

Add lean proteins: skinless chicken, turkey, or tofu.

Introduce well-cooked vegetables (e.g., carrots, zucchini) in small amounts.

Continue small, frequent meals rather than three large ones.

Keep sipping clear fluids and oral rehydration solutions between meals.

Hour 16–24: Stabilize and Monitor

By now, you should notice improvement. To maintain progress:

Incorporate soluble fiber sources: oats, psyllium husk, or chia seeds.

Consider a starter dose of a probiotic supplement (Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG or Saccharomyces boulardii) to help restore normal gut flora.

Avoid artificial sweeteners like sorbitol, which can worsen diarrhea.

Over-the-Counter (OTC) Aids

If hydration and diet alone aren't enough, these OTC options can help:

Loperamide (Imodium): Slows intestinal motility. Follow package directions.

Bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol): Reduces stool frequency; also relieves cramping.

Oral rehydration solutions: Brands like Pedialyte or generic equivalents.

Always read labels for dosing, and do not exceed the recommended amount. If symptoms persist beyond 48 hours despite OTC use, consult your healthcare provider.

Preventing Future Flare-Ups

Titrate Your Dose Slowly

Work with your doctor to increase Ozempic gradually.

Many people tolerate lower starting doses better, then advance every 4–8 weeks.

Lifestyle Tips

Eat smaller, more frequent meals instead of two or three large ones.

Stay upright for at least 30 minutes after eating—gravity helps digestion.

Manage stress with relaxation techniques; anxiety can worsen GI symptoms.

When to Seek Medical Help

While most diarrhea on Ozempic can be managed at home, talk to your doctor if you experience:

Severe abdominal pain or persistent cramping

Signs of dehydration (dizziness, rapid heart rate, very dark urine)

Blood or pus in stools

Fever over 101°F (38.3°C)

Diarrhea lasting more than 48 hours despite home care
