Understanding the types of tests available

Two basic test types are widely available for potential travelers to use. The first is called the antigen test. This is also sometimes referred to as the rapid test. The second testing method is the PCR test.

Antigen

The antigen test currently meets the requirements of the CDC travel restrictions, but these tests may not be accepted overseas. Even if you receive a negative antigen test, there is a potential that you are currently infected with the Covid-19 virus as the results are known to be less accurate.

The RT-PCR test is conducted by using a nasal or throat swab. This approach can be used in individuals who may or may not have symptoms of the virus. While the wait time is slightly longer than the antigen test, the higher accuracy often leaves little doubt that passengers are healthy and ready to travel.

The RT-PCR test is designed to detect the virus’ RNA. Once the test is conducted, the sample is sent to a lab. Medical professionals convert the RNA into DNA at the laboratory and then replicate the DNA to allow for proper identification. This process has to be conducted on an individual sample and takes a few hours to complete rather than just a few minutes. While the additional wait time may seem like an inconvenience, the results are nearly 100% accurate, making the PCR test for traveling abroad a superior option.

Protecting your travel plans

While fast results may tempt many passengers to take a lower accuracy test to increase the odds that they can board their flight, you should consider the long-term effects of a false negative test. The incubation period of Covid-19 generally takes between five to six days, although it can last up to fourteen days. Taking a highly accurate test before you board your flight will allow you to travel with certainty that you’ll be able to complete all of the activities that you have planned.

If you get a false negative but then develop symptoms shortly after you arrive at your destination, you’ll likely be sent into an immediate quarantine until you can be medically cleared. If you are traveling for work, that may mean that you miss critical meetings or appointments because you are in isolation. Additionally, you may not even have adequate internet coverage where you are being quarantined. Reduced coverage can impact your ability to communicate with the outside world.

If you are on a personal vacation, you may risk having yourself, and any other travelers with you quarantined until you are medically cleared. For shorter duration vacations, this may mean that you have to increase the amount of time you are traveling (and increase associated expenses) just so that you are stuck in a room.

In contrast, a properly identified positive case can postpone their trip to isolate in the comfort of their home with access to their regular medical providers. A postponed trip allows you to reschedule and enjoy the entirety of your trip at a slightly later date.
