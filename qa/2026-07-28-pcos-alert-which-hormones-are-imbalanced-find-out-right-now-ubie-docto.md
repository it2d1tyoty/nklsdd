Published on: 7/14/2026

Which hormones are typically imbalanced in individuals with Polycystic Ovary Syndrome?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is commonly linked to hormonal imbalances involving insulin, androgens (such as testosterone), and luteinizing hormone (LH). Elevated insulin can drive the ovaries to produce excess androgens, while high LH levels further disrupt ovulation. Together, these imbalances contribute to the hallmark symptoms of PCOS, including irregular periods, acne, weight changes, and unwanted hair growth.

Explanation

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome, or PCOS, is a condition where certain hormones in the body are not balanced. Hormones that are commonly imbalanced in PCOS include:

Androgens: These are often called male hormones, but everyone has them. In PCOS, the body makes too many androgens, which can cause symptoms like extra hair growth and acne.

[Find Out Right](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/)

Insulin: This hormone helps control blood sugar levels. In PCOS, the body might not use insulin well, leading to higher insulin levels, which can also increase androgen production.

Luteinizing Hormone (LH): This hormone helps control the menstrual cycle. In PCOS, there can be too much LH, which can disrupt normal ovulation.

Understanding these hormone changes is important for managing PCOS. Treatments often focus on balancing these hormones to help reduce symptoms and improve overall health.
