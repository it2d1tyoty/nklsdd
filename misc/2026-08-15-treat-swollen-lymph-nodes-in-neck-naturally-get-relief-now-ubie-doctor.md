Swollen lymph nodes in the neck are typically small, tender lumps that appear when your body is fighting an infection, such as a cold, sore throat, or ear infection. In most cases, they improve on their own within a few weeks with rest, hydration, and treatment of the underlying cause.

However, swollen lymph nodes that persist longer than two weeks, continue to grow, feel hard or fixed, or are accompanied by fever, night sweats, or unexplained weight loss may signal a more serious condition and should be evaluated by a doctor.

Because swollen lymph nodes can stem from many causes—ranging from mild viral infections to conditions requiring prompt care—understanding your specific symptoms is key to knowing what to do next.
