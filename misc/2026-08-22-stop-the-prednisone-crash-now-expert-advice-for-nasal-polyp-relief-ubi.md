What Is a Prednisone Crash?

When you take prednisone, your body's adrenal glands slow or stop producing natural cortisol. If you stop prednisone suddenly—or taper too quickly—your body may struggle to resume normal cortisol production. This leads to a "crash," with symptoms such as:

Extreme tiredness or fatigue after shrinking nasal polyps treatment

Low blood pressure or dizziness

Muscle aches and joint pain

Mood changes (irritability, anxiety or low mood)

Difficulty concentrating or "brain fog"

This adrenal suppression is usually temporary, but it can last days to weeks, depending on dose and duration of your prednisone regimen.

Why Fatigue Occurs

Prednisone crash fatigue after shrinking nasal polyps arises from:

Cortisol imbalance: Your body needs time to restart healthy cortisol levels.

Inflammation rebound: As prednisone levels drop, underlying inflammation can surge.

Sleep disruption: Steroid side effects often interfere with restful sleep.

Nutrient depletion: Long-term prednisone use can deplete calcium, magnesium and vitamin D.

Timeline and What to Expect

Most people notice crash symptoms 24–72 hours after reducing or stopping prednisone. Tapering more slowly can ease the crash, but some fatigue and malaise are common until hormone regulation normalizes. Expect gradual improvement over 1–4 weeks. If symptoms persist or worsen, speak to your doctor.

Practical Steps to Ease the Prednisone Crash

Below is a structured plan you can follow. These strategies are based on current endocrine and respiratory care guidelines.

1. Follow a Safe Tapering Schedule

Always taper under medical supervision.

Typical taper: reduce by 5–10 mg every 1–2 weeks, adjusting based on symptoms.

Never stop abruptly, especially after >2 weeks of prednisone.

2. Prioritize Balanced Nutrition

Eating well supports adrenal recovery and reduces fatigue.

Include lean proteins (chicken, fish, legumes) to rebuild muscle.

Eat whole grains and complex carbohydrates (brown rice, oats) for steady energy.

Add healthy fats (avocado, olive oil, nuts) to support hormone balance.

Load up on fruits and veggies for antioxidants and vitamin C.

Consider calcium-rich foods (yogurt, kale) and vitamin D sources (fatty fish, fortified milk).

3. Stay Hydrated and Mind Your Electrolytes

Prednisone can shift fluid and electrolyte balance, contributing to fatigue.

Aim for 8–10 cups of water daily.

Include electrolyte sources: bananas (potassium), nuts (magnesium), and a pinch of salt in meals.

Herbal teas (chamomile or peppermint) can soothe digestion without caffeine.

4. Rebuild Sleep Hygiene

Quality sleep accelerates recovery of your adrenal axis and reduces fatigue.

Maintain consistent bedtimes and wake-up times, even on weekends.

Wind down 30 minutes before bed: dim lights, avoid screens, and try reading or gentle stretching.

Keep your bedroom cool, dark and quiet.

Avoid caffeine after midday; switch to herbal tea in the afternoon.

5. Introduce Gentle Movement

Exercise can lift mood, boost energy and strengthen muscles without overtaxing your system.

Begin with low-impact activities: walking, gentle yoga or water aerobics.

Start 10–15 minutes per day, gradually increasing time and intensity as tolerated.

When to Seek Medical Attention

Some symptoms require prompt evaluation:

Severe dizziness or fainting

Unexplained weight loss or persistent nausea/vomiting

Confusion or severe mood swings

Chest pain or palpitations

Signs of infection (fever, chills, redness around wounds)

If you experience any of these, contact your healthcare provider immediately.
