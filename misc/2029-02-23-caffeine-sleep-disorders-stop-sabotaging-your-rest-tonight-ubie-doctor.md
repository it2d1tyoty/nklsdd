How does caffeine impact sleep disorders?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Caffeine can make it harder to fall asleep and stay asleep. Because caffeine blocks adenosine (the brain chemical that promotes drowsiness), it delays sleep onset, reduces total sleep time, and lowers deep-sleep quality — particularly when consumed regularly or within 6 hours of bedtime.

Explanation

Caffeine is a stimulant that can affect sleep patterns. It can make it harder to fall asleep and stay asleep. When you drink caffeine, especially close to bedtime, it can keep you awake longer and make your sleep less restful.

[Tonight Ubie Doctor's](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/blog/5100911742.html)

Effects of Caffeine on Sleep:

Delayed Sleep: Caffeine can make it take longer to fall asleep. This means you might go to bed at your usual time but find yourself lying awake.

Less Deep Sleep: Caffeine can reduce the amount of deep sleep you get. Deep sleep is important for feeling rested and refreshed.

REM Sleep Disruption: Regular caffeine use can delay the start of REM sleep, which is the dream stage of sleep.
