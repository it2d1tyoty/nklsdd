How is a weak urine stream defined?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

A weak urine stream means urine flows more slowly or with less force than usual. Common causes include benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), urinary tract infections, bladder muscle weakness, urethral strictures, or nerve-related conditions. In men, an enlarged prostate is the most frequent culprit, while women may experience it due to pelvic floor issues.

Because a weak stream can point to several different conditions—some easily treated, others requiring prompt care—it's important to identify the likely cause early. Explanation

A weak urine stream happens when the flow of urine is slower or less forceful. This can make it hard to empty the bladder completely. One common cause is benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), which is when the prostate gland gets bigger and squeezes the urethra, the tube that carries urine out of the body.

Causes of Weak Urine Stream

Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH): As men age, the prostate can grow larger. This growth can press on the urethra, making it hard for urine to flow out smoothly.

Other Factors: Though BPH is a common cause, other issues like blockages or muscle problems can also lead to a weak stream.

Symptoms to Watch For

Difficulty Starting Urination: It might take longer to start urinating.
