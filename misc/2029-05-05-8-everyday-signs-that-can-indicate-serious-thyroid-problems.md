What is thyroid disease?

The thyroid is a small gland located in the neck. It produces hormones that regulate the body’s metabolism, heart rate, and energy level. When the thyroid is not functioning properly, a person can develop a range of conditions known as thyroid disease.

The two most common types of thyroid disease are hyperthyroidism and hypothyroidism. Hyperthyroidism occurs when the thyroid is overactive, meaning it produces too much of its hormones. This can lead to symptoms such as weight loss, anxiety, rapid heartbeat, and sweating. Hypothyroidism is the opposite; it occurs when the thyroid is underactive, producing too little of its hormones. Symptoms of this condition include fatigue, dry skin, depression, and weight gain.

Certain factors can increase a person’s risk of developing thyroid problems. These include age (older individuals are more likely to have thyroid issues), gender (women are at greater risk than men), family history (if you have a close relative with a thyroid condition, you’re more likely to develop one yourself), and certain medical conditions like diabetes or an autoimmune disorder.
