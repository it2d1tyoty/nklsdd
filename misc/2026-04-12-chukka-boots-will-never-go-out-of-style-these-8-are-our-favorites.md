What to Consider

Upper Material

The upper material dictates not only how the boot looks but also how it wears over time. Here's what you need to know when choosing the right one for you.

Premium Suede

Made from the underside of animal hide, suede has a soft, velvety texture. We recommend that you look for full-grain or high-quality split suede with a rich nap. Either way, suede molds to your foot over time, making it incredibly comfortable. It's also lightweight and flexible, which adds to the relaxed, easygoing feel of a chukka boot.

Full-Grain Leather

Full-grain leather is the most durable and high-quality leather type, taken from the outermost part of the hide. It's tougher than suede, holds its shape better, and offers more protection from the elements—great for year-round use. It also tends to acquire a beautiful patina over time.

Sole Type

The sole of a chukka boot impacts how comfortable it feels, how long it lasts, and where you can wear it. Here's a breakdown of the two main options.

Crepe Rubber (Classic & Most Comfortable)

A natural rubber with a soft, slightly bouncy texture. It provides excellent shock absorption and comfort, making desert boots feel almost like sneakers. A thick, high-quality crepe that isn't too soft—some cheaper versions wear down quickly.

Full Rubber (Best for Wet Conditions)

A standard rubber sole without any crepe material. It's more durable, better traction, and holds up in wet or rough conditions. Look for a high-quality option that isn't stiff—flexibility is key for comfort.

What's the Difference Between Chukka Boots and Desert Boots?

Desert boots are a type of chukka boot, but not all chukka boots are desert boots. Chukka boots are defined by a wide opening created by two panels stitched over the tongue. They have low ankles, usually about the same as a high-top basketball sneaker. Lacing is simplified by four eyelets that make for quick and easy tying. While chukka boots can be made from any kind of material and feature any kind of sole, desert boots are defined by lightweight materials and simplified construction. So the uppers are usually made out of soft leather or suede and the soles are usually made from crepe or soft rubber.

How to Style Desert or Chukka Boots

Think of desert boots as a little more casual than dress shoes and a little more formal than a sneaker. These all-purpose boots look best with jeans and chinos. Most office settings allow for desert boots as an alternative to oxfords or derby shoes. If you find yourself wearing sneakers all the time with your casual outfits, try switching to a pair of desert boots for a more elevated look that doesn’t give up any comfort or ease. Since the opening is wide and the simple four-eyelet lace design is easy to tie, putting them on and taking them off isn’t much more difficult than a slip-on shoe.
